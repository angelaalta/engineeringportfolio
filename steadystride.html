SteadyStride — Self-Stabilizing Cane

Tremor-resistant cane mechanism to reduce stumbles and improve confidence during ambulation.

Role: Mechanical Lead Timeline: 2024–2025 Skills: Mechanisms, prototyping, user testing
← Back to Works

Problem

Hand tremor and uneven ground can destabilize a traditional cane, increasing effort and fall risk.

Outcome

A passive self-stabilizing head that filters high-frequency inputs and auto-levels the tip.

Impact

Prototype demonstrated smoother tip contact and improved perceived stability in pilot tests (n=6).

Overview

SteadyStride is a cane add-on that passively stabilizes the tip using a compact mechanism. The design reduces transmission of high-frequency tremor and helps the tip maintain normal contact with the floor.

My Role

  • Concept generation, mechanism trade studies (flexures vs. bearings vs. compliant linkages)
  • CAD, DFM, and rapid prototyping (SLA/FFF prints, machined parts)
  • Bench testing (step/impulse response) & early hallway/user sessions

Design Requirements

  • Passive (no batteries), compact, retrofittable to standard cane shafts
  • Attenuate 6–12 Hz hand tremor without feeling “mushy” during stance
  • Durable in outdoor conditions; tip must remain grippy and replaceable

Approach

We explored two architectures: a viscously-damped gimbal and a compliant-pivot with tuned stiffness. Early benchtop rigs quantified tip displacement vs. input frequency. Final alpha used a low-profile gimbal with viscoelastic pads providing phase lead and energy dissipation.

Testing

  • Bench: sinusoidal input 2–15 Hz, tip travel < 40% baseline at 8–10 Hz
  • Pilot walks (n=6): perceived smoothness ↑; planting accuracy ↑; learning time < 5 min
  • Durability: 5,000 plant cycles with no loosening; pads replaceable

Status

Alpha prototype complete; next steps include extended trials, pad material optimization, and tool-less service features.

Build Specs

  • Mass: ~280 g add-on
  • Envelope: 62 mm Ø × 45 mm height
  • Materials: PA12, TPU (pads), 6061-T6, stainless fasteners
  • Processes: SLA/FFF print, CNC, laser-cut elastomer
